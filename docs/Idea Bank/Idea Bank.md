## The sequence (ranked 2026-09-12)

This page has opened with "otherwise unprioritized" since 2026-07-12, and that stopped being true today. The ranking is the player's, and so is the criterion: **four axes at once** - what other items are waiting on, what the player feels, learning/portfolio depth, and the plainest design hole - with the rule that **the top is where the axes agree**. An item that wins on three goes ahead of one that wins hard on one, which is what finally settles this page's oldest tie: juice against engineering module.

Two readings came out of the ranking that were not on the page, both the player's:

**The post-run report is an instrument, not only legibility.** [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md) already says the harness and the players are not disagreeing but measuring different things, and that every simulated run is a floor because the harness never uses Overclock. A report measures the run that *does* use Overclock and *does* change its mind. That moves it in front of the roster question rather than beside it: you do not choose between a fifth tower and a tier 4 before measuring.

**And deterministic replay is the third end of the same instrument.** The report is an aggregate - "wave 7 cost 2 core HP". The log is the run itself, so a real run can be played back *inside* `balance.ts`, which closes that gap literally rather than by approximation. Three things fall out of that. The **adaptive layout** loses its main justification, because it existed as a proxy for "a line that changes its mind" and a recorded human run is not a proxy - but it survives as the *regression gate*, because a replay is scoped to the curve that produced it, which is v1.7's `CURVE_ID` lesson one floor up: retune the curve and every stored replay is about another game. So replay measures and the adaptive layout guards the gate. The **determinism gate goes first**, because the whole milestone assumes the game and the harness are one simulation, and today that is a claim rather than a test - if they have silently diverged, every number in the milestone is wrong. And **save and resume separates**, because it is comfort rather than measurement, and rides along as the cuttable tail.

### The six

1. **The run as data, and what it measures** - deterministic replay and the determinism gate first, then Overclock in the sim, the post-run report and leak attribution, with save/resume as the cuttable tail. *Does what the player does match what the harness assumes - and are the two simulations still one?* **Promoted to v1.9** (2026-09-12): build order in [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md). v1.8 is what made it urgent rather than merely next - that milestone's whole reading came from a script that never fires Overclock and never changes its mind, on a board whose thesis is a placement decision.
2. **The fourth question** - the [Roster](../Design/Roster.md) rule written down, and the answer it picks: a fifth tower, a tier 4, or neither. Only decidable after 1, and it unblocks adjacency, loadout and tier 4 at once. *Three new questions arrived in v1.6 - which of them deserves a new answer?*
3. **What the hand does** - the economy sink, a second active ability, or the one that turns out to be both. Behind 2 because it is the smaller hole, and it stays there: the condition written here was "if real runs end with more surplus than `veteran`'s 164 unspent Cycles", and v1.9 step 6 measured one. **It came back no.** The 164 was a ten-wave reading and v1.6 had already moved it to 62; against the curve that exists, the recorded human run banks 64 to `veteran`'s 62, runs down to 5 Cycles at wave 6, and the rows banking the most in that table are all breaches - surplus mostly measures how early a run stopped being able to spend. The adaptive layout lands here as the regression gate. *Past the midpoint, does the player choose again?*
4. **The board explains itself** - health bars, hit flash, floating +Cycles, tier sprites, a wave preview that states the question, and the palette substitution. This is where the art pass happens, and grouped this way it has a system spine instead of being only sprites. The Legibility trio below is **split on purpose**: the report and leak attribution are measurement and went to 1, while the preview is teaching and belongs here. *Is the board read at a glance, or does it have to be explained?*
5. **The board as a participant** - tiles with properties, probably the second spawn port with it. The lever v1.8 left on the table when it chose the fold as its axis. *Is a third board a third problem or a third drawing?*
6. **A\* + maze mode** - the only item on this page that is another game rather than this one's next piece, and the one thing here that would justify the project turning the corner into a v2. Nothing in `docs/` has ever written a v2 down.

**And one branch rather than a slot: endless.** v1.8's verify asks whether two records read as two games or as one game split in half. If it comes back split, endless jumps the queue by the roadmap's own words. It cannot be scheduled ahead of that answer.

### What stayed out, and why

Login is still a different project rather than a next step, which [Before the Run](./Before%20the%20Run.md) argues at length. Difficulty modes are an admitted knob and lose to item 2. Loadout waits on item 2, because "take three of the four" is thin while there are four. First-run tutorial hints are absorbed by the preview in item 4. The adaptive music layers wait on stems that do not exist. The portrait-shaped map waits on the viewport debt v1.8 declared out of scope. Adjacency, armor, stealth, healer, EMP, the fifteen-wave second act, modifier runs, the sprite editor and the render perf pass are all standing and none rejected - they are either the material items 2 and 3 get built out of, or the cheap end this page keeps noting a milestone can absorb on the way past.

**This is a sequence and not a schedule.** [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md) is what records a chosen milestone and the date it was chosen on, and nothing above has been chosen.

## Gameplay depth

- Tower upgrades - **promoted to v1.2** (2026-08-23): specced in [Tower Panel](../Design/Tower%20Panel.md), build order in [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md).
- Sell/refund towers - **promoted to v1.2** (2026-08-23), same note.
- Wave preview + early-call bonus - **promoted to v1.3** (2026-08-23): specced in [Run Structure](../Design/Run%20Structure.md), build order in [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md). Both shipped 2026-08-24, and the call left one thing behind, below.
- Give the early call a mechanical cost (2026-08-24) - as shipped it is free money for anyone with a plan: Cycles come only from kills and the countdown only starts once the board is clear, so the skipped seconds were not producing anything, and the harness measures identical defense with +77 Cycles. Its intended cost is reaction time, which a scripted player has infinitely much of. Two candidates, both rejected as out of scope mid-milestone: start the countdown when a wave finishes *spawning* rather than when the board clears (retunes the whole curve), or allow the call during `waiting-clear` so an early wave overlaps the stragglers still walking - an opt-in risk, and the more interesting of the two. **Carried into [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md)** (2026-08-28): the first candidate turns out to be the largest difficulty lever available, so what was filed here as the call's missing cost is read there as the curve's missing pressure. **Promoted to v1.6** (2026-08-30) as the first of the two candidates: it lands there as "Overlapping waves", and the cost this note says the call is missing is the thing that step exists to give it.
- New enemy behaviors - stealth (would re-justify IDS Scanner's dropped "reveals stealth" ability), healer/repair-bot, shielded enemy, EMP unit that briefly disables the nearest tower (mirrors Overclock's overheat). **Carried into [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md)** (2026-08-28), with a fifth added there: a kind immune to slow, which would punish the aura build the way the Zero-Day punishes the all-turret build. **Promoted to v1.6** (2026-08-30): immune-to-slow and shielded are the two the milestone names, and the trait table it refactors before them is the seam the rest of this list plugs into.
- Endless mode - after the scripted waves, procedurally scale further waves; gives high scores something to measure. Held out of v1.3 on purpose: it measures a run against a standard, and v1.3 is building the standard. **Carried into [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md)** (2026-08-28): the standard exists now, and endless is the answer to "I finished it and it was easy" that does not require the curve to change. Named out of scope in v1.6 (2026-08-30) as structure rather than difficulty, and left standing: the run-wide scalar that milestone builds to measure margin is also what an endless ramp would multiply. Picked up again by [Before the Run](./Before%20the%20Run.md) (2026-08-30) from the other end: endless is a *second kind of run*, and the reason it has nowhere to be chosen is that the game has no before. **Taken off the critical path** (2026-08-31): a second map is also a second entry in the picker, and a different kind of choice, so endless no longer has to exist before a chooser is a chooser. It stays where it belongs, as the answer to what a record measures. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: not a slot but the branch - see [The sequence](#the-sequence-ranked-2026-09-12).
- Towers have not changed since v1 (2026-08-31) - the plainest design hole on the page: v1.6 added three enemy kinds, which is three new questions, and zero new answers. The fix is not automatically a fifth tower. It is the [Roster](../Design/Roster.md) rule pointed the other way - **every tower answers a question no other tower answers** - written down once, because that is the thing that decides between a fifth tower, a tier 4 (already filed in [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md)) and neither. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 2, and only decidable after item 1 measures.
- Adjacency (2026-08-31) - the board is a grid and the only spatial rule in the game is the range circle. A tower that strengthens the one next to it, or a penalty for clustering, makes *placement* a decision rather than a consequence of range. It aims at the axis act two of the curve already chose when it scaled speed instead of HP: coverage, not damage.
- A second active ability (2026-08-31) - Overclock is the player's only verb while a wave is on the board, and "past the midpoint I stop choosing" ([Difficulty](./Difficulty.md)) is as much about that as it is about Cycles. An ability bought with Cycles is also the economy sink v1.6 left out, arriving as something to *do* rather than something to repair. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 3, together with the economy sink [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md) left standing.
- Loadout chosen before the run (2026-08-31) - take three of the four towers in. Variety without new content, and the harness already models exactly this: its seven declared layouts are loadouts. It is also the first real answer to [Before the Run](./Before%20the%20Run.md)'s complaint that a picker with one item is worse than a start button, because a loadout is a choice that exists today. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: left out of the sequence, waiting on item 2 - taking three of the four is thin while there are four.
- Modifier runs and a daily seed (2026-08-31) - a run with a declared twist (no IDS Scanner, double speed and double bounty, one core HP). The plumbing is already built and nothing on the game side is wired to it: the run-wide scalar the margin bisect added is a modifier that only the harness can set.

## Content

- Map 2+ - the waypoint-only level format ([Map Layout](../Design/Map%20Layout.md)) was designed for this; add a map-select screen. Layout candidates: fork/merge path, spiral. **Named as the milestone after v1.7** (2026-08-31): the shell is what gives a second map somewhere to be chosen, and the map is what gives the shell a second thing to choose. "Tiles with properties" below is what would make it play differently rather than look different. **Shipped as v1.8** (2026-09-12), as neither candidate: the axis is a trace that **folds back on itself**, so a tower in the corridor between the legs covers both. And the milestone found the thing this line assumed - that a longer trace is a harder board - is false. Trace nothing covers is enemies walking for free; coverage is difficulty and length is not. See [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md).
- Real wave design pass - **promoted to v1.3** (2026-08-23), same note. 4 waves is a demo; a ~10-wave curve with deliberate difficulty pacing makes balancing a real (and instructive) discipline. Shipped, and the curve it produced is what [Difficulty](./Difficulty.md) is now reading player feedback against.
- A second spawn port, opening mid-run (2026-08-31) - the port is already a control the player taps, the level format is waypoints, and a second one opening in act two is a structural climax that costs no new enemy kind. The part worth arguing about is whether it opens on a schedule or as the price of something. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 5.
- Tiles with properties (2026-08-31) - a tile that grants range, a burned tile that cannot be built on, a tile only auras may hold. The cheapest way to make a second map *play* differently instead of look different, which is the thing map 2 has to earn. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 5, which is where the lever v1.8 did not pull ends up. **And v1.8 sharpened what it would be for**: with length measured as worth nothing and coverage as worth everything, a tile with a property is a *second* way to change coverage - the only other one this game has.

## Feel / juice

- Web Audio - SFX (fire, impact, glitch-kill, wave klaxon) + low ambient synth loop. Big learning module. **Split across three milestones** (2026-08-27): the desk and one composed track are v1.5 (see [Audio](../Design/Audio.md)); the four adaptive layers wait on stems that do not exist yet; the SFX are their own milestone after, because polyphony - voice cap, retrigger floor, pitch jitter - is a real subsystem and the note that argued for synthesis over samples still holds.
- Path pulse animation - data packets flowing along the trace; sells the PCB theme for little cost.
- Hit feedback - enemy flash on hit, health bars (or damage-state color shift), floating +Cycles on kill, subtle screen shake on core breach. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 4, and the health bar is read there as legibility rather than juice - the curve spent ten waves on HP the player cannot see.
- Entity sprite/art pass - **promoted to v1.1** (2026-07-12): specced in [Sprite Spec](../Design/Sprite%20Spec.md), build order in [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md).
- **Tower sprites that improve with each upgrade** (player, 2026-08-31) - a deferral coming due rather than a new idea. [Tower Panel](../Design/Tower%20Panel.md) refused tier art in v1.2 with a condition attached: tiers read procedurally and "graduate to real sprites only if the procedural tell looks cheap in play". The player has now called it, which is that condition being met, and `render.ts` says the same thing in a comment beside the pips it draws. What it buys is a board readable at a glance - today a maxed board and a fresh one are the same picture with a few pips on it, which is the wrong picture for the decision [Tower Panel](../Design/Tower%20Panel.md) made central. The v1.2 estimate of "three sprites per tower" is also the wrong number now: towers are drawn from primitives in `scripts/gen-sprites.mjs`, so a tier is a parameter to a shape function the same way `firewallNode(frame)` already parameterizes animation. The question [Sprite Spec](../Design/Sprite%20Spec.md) settles rather than taste: silhouette already encodes *function* (dish = aura, barrel = single-target), so tier has to ride on something else - added detail, a brighter core, more segments - or the ladder starts overwriting what the shape is for. **Considered for v1.7 and left out** (2026-08-31): it blocks nothing and nothing blocks it, which makes it the milestone to take when the next one should be art rather than system. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 4, grouped with the hit feedback and the palette so the art pass has a system spine.
- Audio as information, not only juice (2026-08-31) - a distinct spawn sound per enemy kind says what is coming without the player taking their eyes off the board. The ROOTKIT makes it more than polish: while it is unrevealed it is the only thing in the game the player cannot see, so sound is the only channel it has left. Sharpens the SFX milestone's brief, which is currently a list of events (fire, impact, glitch-kill, klaxon) rather than a list of readings.

## Mobile / UX

- Physical-device test pass - **done in v1.2's verify step** (2026-08-23), together with the QA owed from v1.1. It stopped being the highest-risk unknown here and became a habit: every milestone verifies on a phone.
- Tap-tower info panel - **shipped in v1.2** (2026-08-23): see [Tower Panel](../Design/Tower%20Panel.md). Now the surface everything else reads through - v1.3's wave preview is its fourth mode.
- Pause + 2x speed toggle - **promoted to v1.3** (2026-08-23): near-mandatory TD quality-of-life, and the tool the ten-wave curve gets tuned with.
- Portrait layout - **promoted to v1.4** (2026-08-25): specced in [Portrait Layout](../Design/Portrait%20Layout.md), build order in [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md). Was the debt the 2026-08-23 log accepted when the build menu was moved to the left edge and portrait was left with no side band to tuck into.
- Rotate the board 90 degrees in portrait - **promoted into v1.4** (2026-08-25), same note. Filed and promoted the same day: it started as the more interesting half of the problem and became the whole answer once the arithmetic was on the table. A 9x16 blit fits at scale 4 and gives back the *full landscape board area* (384x683, 42px tiles) on a phone held upright, where the unrotated board gets a quarter of it.
- Portrait-shaped map (2026-08-25) - the 9x16 level the waypoint format would already take, which is the version of the above where nothing reads sideways. Held out of v1.4 for the reason v1.3 gave for refusing map 2: a second map inheriting a curve should inherit a finished one.
- First-run tutorial hints - portfolio visitors give it ~30 seconds; a "place a tower here" nudge decides whether they see the game at all. **Challenged 2026-08-31** by the Legibility category below: the same 30 seconds are also bought by a preview that says what a wave is *asking*, which the console can say without inventing a tutorial layer on top of it. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: absorbed by that preview in sequence item 4 rather than scheduled on its own.
- **Tell the player a new build has arrived** (2026-08-28) - found while answering "how does the installed app update on my phone". Today it takes **two launches**: `sw.js` carries `skipWaiting()` and `clientsClaim()`, so a new service worker installs and takes control immediately, but the generated `registerSW.js` is the bare registration with no reload, and the page that is already open keeps running the JS it loaded. So launch one fetches the update and launch two shows it. (The `max-age=600` GitHub Pages puts on everything does not add to that: `updateViaCache` defaults to `imports`, so the top-level worker script skips the HTTP cache on every update check. The workaround meanwhile is to hard-refresh the same URL in a browser tab, which updates the worker for the installed app too, since it is one registration per origin and scope.)
  **Auto-reloading is the wrong fix**, which is why this is a note and not a one-liner: a run is six to eight minutes, and reloading during wave 9 destroys it. The two candidates are reloading only when nothing is at stake (no run started, or the end screen is up) and a quiet "new build" marker the player taps when they choose. The second is more this project's idiom - the console already narrates state, and the decision belongs to whoever is holding the phone.

## Legibility - the game explaining itself (2026-08-31)

New category. The [Roster](../Design/Roster.md) is a table of questions the game never asks out loud, and v1.6's verify step is left holding the ones no harness can answer - whether the ROOTKIT wall was *understood*, whether act two reads as a rhythm. The three below are one idea at three moments: before a wave, during it, and after the run.

- A post-run report - which wave took core HP, which kind crossed and how many, the peak enemy count on the board. Not a score, a reading. It is also the material a score would later be made of, which is the gap [Before the Run](./Before%20the%20Run.md) names between a verdict and a number. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 1, and promoted on the reading that it is an *instrument* - it measures the run that uses Overclock and changes its mind, which is the one thing the harness cannot.
- Leak attribution as it happens - the core loses HP today and nothing names what did it, in a console that narrates everything else. One line per breach. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 1, as the per-breach data the report aggregates. One line at `game.ts:286`.
- A wave preview that states the question, not the count - "ROOTKIT: needs IDS coverage" beside the kinds that are coming. This is the first-run tutorial (Mobile / UX) in the project's own idiom, and probably a better one: the console is already the voice of the mainframe, while a "place a tower here" nudge is a different game's voice. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 4, deliberately split from the other two - those are measurement, this is teaching.

## Accessibility (2026-08-31)

New category, and it exists because nothing in `docs/` had mentioned accessibility once. That is the reason to open it, not a claim that anything below is urgent.

- The palette is green and magenta on near-black, close to the worst available pair for the most common color blindness, and magenta is the one carrying *damage* - the signal that has to land. [Theme](../Design/Theme.md) keeps the palette strict and small and the pixel-strings are palette-mapped by construction, so this is a table substitution rather than an art pass. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 4, where a table substitution rides along with the art pass.
- Reduced motion - the glitch-scatter kill, the marching chevrons on the armed port, and the screen shake the hit-feedback bullet wants. `prefers-reduced-motion` is one media query, and the effects already live in a module of their own.

## Engineering / learning modules (the project's actual driver)

- A* pathfinding + maze mode - towers block the path, enemies re-route; best isolated learning module here. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 6, last in the queue and not in the bank - it is the one item here that is another game.
- Balance sim harness - **promoted to v1.3** (2026-08-23), same note. Cheaper than it looked: the sim is already deterministic (the only `Math.random()` is in cosmetic particles), so the cost is extracting it out of `main.ts`'s closure, which is its own win.
- Render perf pass - pre-render the static board to an offscreen canvas, object-pool projectiles/particles; addresses the low-end-mobile risk flagged in [Architecture](../Design/Architecture.md). Board prerender partially lands with the v1.1 sprite pass renderer step.
- Sprite editor mini-tool - tiny browser editor that round-trips the pixel-string sprite format ([Sprite Spec](../Design/Sprite%20Spec.md)); only worth it if text-editing sprites chafes after v1.1 - the dev gallery view covers most of the need.
- Deterministic replay (2026-08-31) - a seed plus an input log is a whole run in a few bytes, and the property it needs is already established and already load-bearing: the only `Math.random()` is in cosmetic particles, which is what made the v1.3 harness possible in the first place. Three payoffs from one module - a bug report from a phone that is not here becomes reproducible, determinism gets a gate instead of a claim, and a run becomes something the player can hand to someone else. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 1, and *first* inside it - the milestone assumes the game and the harness are one simulation, and the gate is what turns that from a claim into a test. **Promoted to v1.9** the same day: build order in [Roadmap](../Roadmap.md).
- Save and resume a run in progress (2026-08-31) - a run is six to eight minutes on a device that can receive a phone call, and today an interruption costs the whole thing. Falls out of the replay log almost free, or out of serializing `GameState` directly. Nothing in these notes had raised it, and it is the most mobile-real item on this page. **Ranked 2026-09-12**: sequence item 1, as the cuttable tail - it falls out of the replay log, but it is comfort rather than measurement. **Promoted to v1.9** the same day, and it went in still marked as the tail - the first thing to go if the milestone runs long.

## Meta / persistence

- Local high scores (best wave / score) via localStorage. **Carried into [Before the Run](./Before%20the%20Run.md)** (2026-08-30), which is the note about the screen they would be read on - and which argues they are coupled to endless, because a fixed curve gives a verdict rather than a score and a record is a statement about a curve that keeps being retuned.
- Settings (sound toggle) once audio exists - **shipped in v1.5** (2026-08-27), and it turned out to be a property of the graph rather than a screen: one gain on a bus, one button in the top band, one `localStorage` key. It is also the project's first `localStorage`, so it is the seam local high scores plug into.
